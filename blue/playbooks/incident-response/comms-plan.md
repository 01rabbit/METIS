# Communicate incident decisions through an authorized channel

Use this decision guide with the [response contract](../README.md). It is independently authored guidance; organization-specific exercise and approval are still required.

## Entry condition

An incident or credible urgent lead requires coordination, a status update or an externally meaningful statement.

## Responsibilities

The incident owner approves technical content and cadence. The designated communications owner manages recipients and channels. Legal/privacy determines applicable notification obligations using the actual facts and jurisdiction.

## Work and decision records

1. Identify audiences and the decision each needs: operators need actions and dependencies; leadership needs impact and options; affected people need approved practical guidance.
2. Record the actual recipients, channel, classification, sender and authorization. A generic response task does not by itself authorize sending a message to another person.
3. Prepare a concise statement separating confirmed effects, credible risks, unresolved scope and the next update time. Avoid speculative attribution and unsupported claims of containment or no data loss.
4. Use approved out-of-band channels if the normal identity or messaging environment may be affected. Preserve channel access controls and recipient verification.
5. Track corrections explicitly. A later scope revision should state what changed in the assessment and what action recipients should take.
6. Provide the relevant facts to the legal/privacy owner early. Do not invent a universal reporting deadline or postpone the obligation review until technical closure.

## Completion evidence

The approved communication has an accountable sender, audience, record of delivery and next update/closure condition. Drafting and sending are separate task states. Raw secrets and unnecessary personal details are excluded.

## Tabletop check

Tabletop an uncertain scope, a wrong initial estimate, unavailable corporate email and a request to notify an external party without a grant. The correct output may be a reviewed draft awaiting authorized delivery.

## Concrete observation contract

Maintain a private audience/contact matrix with role, primary and alternate channel,
recipient verification, sending authority, trigger, deadline and next-update time.
Record the source of each deadline and the event that starts its clock. Notification
requirements depend on applicable contract/jurisdiction and authorized advice; do not
turn a familiar number into a universal legal rule.

Each message record should identify the case, version, approved sender, intended
audience, classification/sharing restriction, delivery time and acknowledgement where
required. Distinguish delivery failure from recipient silence. Keep factual updates,
estimates and decisions requested separate, with the next expected update even when
uncertainty persists. Contact details, secrets and raw evidence stay in private storage;
public lessons must not reproduce the incident's communications.
