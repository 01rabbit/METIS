# Assign decision ownership across the response program

Use this decision guide with the [response contract](../README.md). It is independently authored guidance; organization-specific exercise and approval are still required.

## Entry condition

A team needs a usable ownership model before or during coordinated incident response.

## Responsibilities

Assign accountable people for incident coordination, evidence, technical changes, service recovery, communication and notification obligations. One person may fill multiple roles in a small team, but each decision still needs an explicit owner and delegate.

## Work and decision records

1. Choose a lifecycle vocabulary that the organization uses and identify its actual decision gates. Do not spend an active incident reconciling labels at the expense of action.
2. Treat preparation as ongoing governance, inventory, protective controls, telemetry and exercised authority. A written contact list is not a tested response capability.
3. Assign a decision owner and consulted specialists for declaration, containment, remediation and return to service. Technical recommendation and business-risk acceptance can belong to different people.
4. Start communication and notification-obligation assessment in parallel with technical work. These activities do not wait for a completed forensic report.
5. Define handover and delegation for unavailable owners and exhausted resources. The agent owner coordinates worker dispatch but does not acquire human business authority by delegation.
6. Link improvement work back into preparedness with acceptance criteria, deadlines and retesting. Closure should not erase open control deficiencies.

## Completion evidence

The team can name who decides each consequential action, who performs it and how unresolved disagreements are escalated. Framework alignment supports those decisions; it is not a claim of compliance.

## Tabletop check

Tabletop absent decision makers, competing service priorities and a critical action with no existing grant. Validate that the escalation path works and that urgent protection can proceed within established authority.

## Concrete observation contract

Build an actual decision matrix with decision type, accountable owner, alternate,
authorized executor, consulted parties, notification audience, authority source and
escalation time. Include service isolation, identity/session changes, evidence acquisition,
restoration and external communication as distinct decisions. A person who supplies
evidence need not own the business decision, and a worker's tool access is not a grant.

Bind each exercised decision to a case record and the applicable scope/expiry. Record an
unavailable owner and the approved fallback instead of inventing authority. NIST's
risk-management framing and the organization's chosen phase vocabulary must map to the
same concrete responsibilities; a framework name does not assign those roles. A tabletop
should test an alternate owner and a failed action, preserving who actually decided and
what remained untested.
